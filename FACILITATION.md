## Two discussions that are not in the notebook

Both used to be questions on screen. They work better spoken, with the numbers visible in the notebook behind you. Give one minute in pairs, then take answers; do not read the points out before they have tried.

**Cracks in concrete (end of section 2, about 3 minutes).** Say: "A colleague segments cracks in concrete and sends you four numbers. SAM with one box: IoU 0.31. Marking everything as foreground: 0.28. A linear head at 512 px: 0.35, and at 1024 px: 0.48. And that 0.48 head has a clDice of 0.22. Use what is on your screen from this section (the grey 'everything' line, the 512 versus 1024 chart, IoU next to clDice). Which two or three signs tell you the foundation model is not good enough out of the box?" Answer points:
- SAM's 0.31 sits next to the 0.28 baseline that knows nothing: it learnt very little about cracks.
- Going from 512 to 1024 px moves the head by 0.13: the cracks are thinner than a patch, so the representation, not the labels, is the bottleneck.
- IoU 0.48 next to clDice 0.22: a pixel metric and a structure metric disagree, so the mask misses branches.
- And always: a crisp mask is not a correct one, so look at it next to the truth.

**The invented pilot (end of section 6, about 3 minutes).** Say: "All three heads so far cost the same strokes. You can add one more afternoon, about four hours of work. A small pilot, with invented numbers, says two things. A: a second round of strokes on the same five images would lift the scribble head's held-out IoU by about 0.04. B: a SAM fine-tuned on retinas would lift the pseudo-labels' IoU on the training images from 0.45 to 0.65, but nobody has measured the head. Which would you pick? What does the table on your screen suggest? What would you measure first?" Answer points:
- Not like for like: A's +0.04 is on the head, B's +0.20 is on the pseudo-labels only. A better teacher helps the head only as far as its mistakes stop being systematic.
- A head trained on pseudo-labels is capped by how well the teacher understands *this* structure: SAM thinks in objects and Grad-CAM in tiles, neither in thin lines, so better pseudo-labels need a better teacher for this domain, not a cleverer threshold.
- More strokes are true information, but they add no coverage between the strokes.
- Measure first: the pseudo-labels against the truth on a few fully labelled images (the diagnosis line in 6.1). If they are close, a better teacher is the lever; if they are far, spend the afternoon on strokes.
